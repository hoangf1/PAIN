## What the numbers show

- **The code is high at every point, in thrust and power alike.** The efficiency J·C_T/C_P stays close: measured 0.913, computed 0.897 at the median; largest difference 0.063.
- **It is a shift along the advance-ratio axis.** The code gives the measured C_T at an advance ratio 0.5 % to 4.9 % higher (median 3.1 %). On the falling part of the curves, where all 38 points lie, that shift is a large relative difference: it grows from about 7 % to over 80 % as the thrust falls towards zero.
- **The points are the ill-conditioned ones.** The digitizer could only read the falling part of each curve (the crowded and shallow parts are not readable by script), so every point lies at or above the advance ratio of best efficiency. A relative tolerance there tests the position of the curve's foot, not the load level.
- **Cause not established.** A shift of this size corresponds to roughly one degree of blade angle (an estimate from the helix angle at 0.75 R, not a fit). Candidates, none tested: the reference line of the printed blade angle, blade twist under load, the zero-lift angle of the XFOIL polars, the tunnel Mach correction. Nothing was changed after the run. Ticket: ISSUE-043.

## Not covered
